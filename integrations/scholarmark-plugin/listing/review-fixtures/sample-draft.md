Volunteer turnover is a persistent challenge for community gardens. In one survey, most volunteers stopped participating within their first year (Riverside Garden Collective, 2024, p. 3).

Mentoring appears to matter: volunteers paired with a mentor "were far more likely to remain active after one year" (Riverside Garden Collective, 2024, p. 3).

Schedule conflicts were the most common reason for leaving, cited by 46 percent of respondents (Riverside Garden Collective, 2024, p. 4).

Coordinators can therefore improve retention by offering shorter shifts (Riverside Garden Collective, 2024, p. 4).

Nationally, garden volunteer programs lose half of their members each year.

Machine learning methods could help coordinators forecast volunteer demand (LeCun et al., 2016).
