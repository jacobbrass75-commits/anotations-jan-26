Web sources are where citation templates break. The page has no byline, or no date, or only a "last updated" stamp. All three major styles have clear rules for this. They just differ.

**First, check whether you should cite the page at all.** Missing authorship and dating are often signs of weak sources. For a thesis, a page with neither may be fine for background but risky as evidence for a central claim.

## Step 1: Look harder before declaring something missing

- **Author:** check the page footer, the "About" page, and the PDF properties if it's a report. Many pages are authored by the organization that publishes them, in which case the organization is the author, not "unknown."
- **Date:** look for "published," "posted," "last updated," and copyright lines, and check the page source or an attached PDF's cover.
- **Title:** use the page's own heading, not the browser tab text if they differ.

## APA 7

APA's [missing reference information page](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/missing-information) gives a table of solutions:

| Missing | Reference entry | In-text |
|---|---|---|
| Author | Title. (Date). Source. | (Title, year) |
| Date | Author. (n.d.). Title. Source. | (Author, n.d.) |
| Title | Author. (Date). [Description of work]. Source. | (Author, year) |
| Author and date | Title. (n.d.). Source. | (Title, n.d.) |

Also from that page:
- **Don't use "Anonymous"** unless the work is actually signed "Anonymous."
- **In-text citations use the year only**, even if the reference gives a full date.
- If the source itself can't be recovered, cite it as a personal communication or find another work.

For URLs, APA's [DOIs and URLs page](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/dois-urls) says to give the URL as a hyperlink, with no "Retrieved from" before it.

**Worked example (invented page):**

> Gulf Coast Historical Society. (n.d.). *Dockworkers' oral histories*. `https://www.example.org/oral-histories`

In-text: (Gulf Coast Historical Society, n.d.)

Here the organization is the author, so the entry starts with its name.

## MLA 9

- **No author:** start the entry with the title.
- **No date:** omit the date element. MLA's [answer on undated web sources](https://style.mla.org/web-sites-no-publication-date/) says to omit it and suggests an access date.
- **Access date:** optional. MLA's [access-date answer](https://style.mla.org/access-dates/) (written for the eighth edition) calls it especially useful when there's no publication date or the page may change. It goes at the end.

**Worked example (same invented page):**

> "Dockworkers' Oral Histories." *Gulf Labor Archive*, Gulf Coast Historical Society, `www.example.org/oral-histories`. Accessed 3 Oct. 2026.

In-text: ("Dockworkers' Oral Histories")

For how the title, container, and publisher elements fit together, see [MLA 9 Works Cited: the core elements](/guides/mla-9-works-cited-core-elements).

## Chicago 18

Purdue OWL's [CMOS 18 web sources page](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/web_sources.html) gives the note pattern:

> Firstname Lastname, "Title of Web Page," Name of Website, Publishing Organization, publication or revision date if available, access date if no other date is available, URL.

Key points from that page:
- **Use an access date only if no other date is available.** Access dates aren't required for formally published electronic sources.
- **If there's a revision date** ("last updated"), use it.
- Access dates go immediately before the URL.
- In the bibliography, an entry with no author begins with the title (per OWL's [NB overview](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/chicago_manual_of_style_18th_edition.html)).

**Worked example (same invented page):**

> 3. "Dockworkers' Oral Histories," Gulf Labor Archive, Gulf Coast Historical Society, accessed October 3, 2026, `https://www.example.org/oral-histories`.

## Side-by-side summary

| Situation | APA | MLA | Chicago |
|---|---|---|---|
| No author | Title moves to author position | Start with title | Start with title |
| No date | (n.d.) | Omit; access date optional | Access date, if no other date |
| Only "last updated" | Use that date | Use that date | Use as revision date |

**When an organization is both author and publisher,** the styles handle the repetition differently. Check your style manual's examples for group authors before you finalize these entries.

## Pages change: capture what you saw

Web pages get edited or removed, and an access date only tells the reader *when* you looked. To protect your evidence:

- **Save the passage you rely on** with the URL and the date you captured it.
- **Note "last updated" dates** as you see them.
- **Consider an archived snapshot** for pages central to your argument.
