An archive day can produce 400 phone photos and, a month later, no idea which photo goes with which folder, or whether a quote you typed matches the page. Archival and scanned sources need more organization than published ones, because no publisher has done it for you.

This guide covers the workflow from camera to quotable, citable text.

## Before you go: check the rules

Archives set their own policies on photography, flash, and reuse. Check the reading room's rules, and ask if you're unsure. Note any required credit line, because some archives specify how their collections should be cited.

## At the archive: photograph for citation, not just reading

**1. Photograph the context first.** Before the first page of each folder, photograph:
- the box and folder label,
- the collection name and call number,
- any finding-aid reference sheet.

These photos become the dividers that tell you where every following image came from.

**2. One page per photo, whole page in frame.** Include the edges, any page or folio number, and stamps or annotations. Cropping off a page number is the most common way to make a photo uncitable.

**3. Shoot flat and evenly lit.** Use no flash if it's prohibited, avoid shadows from your phone, and keep the page square to the camera. OCR accuracy depends heavily on this.

**4. Photograph backs** if anything is written there, even a date or a filing note.

**5. Keep a running log** in a notes app: "Box 4 / Folder 12 / Hollis to Commissioners, 3 Mar 1911, 3 sheets, photos 118–121." That log is your safety net if photo order gets scrambled.

## After: assemble documents, then OCR

**Group photos into documents.** A three-sheet letter should become *one* source, not three, with the sheets in the right order. Otherwise "sheet 2" means nothing six weeks later.

**Name each document from your log,** for example: `1911-03-03 Hollis to Harbor Commissioners (Box 4, F12)`.

**Then run OCR.** OCR turns the image into searchable text. That's what makes a 400-photo archive searchable, and what lets you copy quotes rather than retype them.

## Check OCR before you quote it

OCR text is a draft transcription. Common errors:

- **Look-alike characters:** "rn" read as "m," "1" as "l," "0" as "O."
- **Old typefaces:** the long s (ſ) misread as "f," and ligatures split or merged.
- **Handwriting:** often unreliable. Treat handwritten OCR as a finding aid, not a transcription.
- **Layout:** columns read across instead of down, footnotes merged into body text, and headers repeated mid-page.
- **Damage:** stains and fold lines produce invented characters.

**The rule:** before quoting, compare the OCR text against the image, word by word. Quote the image's wording. If you silently correct the OCR, you're fine. If the original itself contains an error, keep it and mark it (see [changing quotations honestly](/guides/changing-quotations-ellipses-brackets)).

## Citing it

Archival citations identify the item and where it's held. A Chicago-style note for the letter above might read:

> 7. J. R. Hollis to the Board of Harbor Commissioners, March 3, 1911, box 4, folder 12, Harbor Commission Records, Galveston County Archives.

*(Invented example.)* Use your archive's preferred citation form if it provides one, and follow your style manual for the details.

Record **box, folder, collection, and repository** at capture time, in the source's metadata or notes. You can't reconstruct them from the photo of a letter alone.

## Worked example: one archive day

**Morning:** 3 boxes, 9 folders. You photograph each folder's label, then each document page by page, logging as you go: 212 photos total.

**Evening:** you sort photos into 26 documents using the folder-label photos as dividers, name each document from your log, and note box and folder in each one's record.

**Next day:** you run OCR on all 26. You read through the 8 documents most relevant to your chapter, comparing OCR text with images, and fix or flag problem passages.

**Over the following weeks:** you search all 26 by keyword and find a 1912 reference to "the Galveston scale" in a document you hadn't flagged. Before quoting, you check the passage against the image and correct one OCR error ("scaie" to "scale").

## Checklist

- [ ] Folder-label photo before each folder's contents.
- [ ] Every page photographed whole, with its number visible.
- [ ] Running log matching photo ranges to documents.
- [ ] Multi-page documents grouped into one source each, in order.
- [ ] Box, folder, collection, and repository recorded for each document.
- [ ] OCR checked against the image before any quotation.
