Each style sets a length threshold for block quotations, and they don't measure length the same way. APA counts words. MLA counts lines in your paper. Chicago uses both. Here are all three side by side, followed by formatting, citation placement, and a checklist.

## Thresholds

| Style | Becomes a block quote when... | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **APA 7** | the quotation is **40 words or more** | [APA Style: Quotations](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/quotations) |
| **MLA 9** | the quotation runs to **more than four lines** of prose in your text (or more than three lines of verse) | [MLA Style Center](https://style.mla.org/font-type-size-block-quotations/); [Purdue OWL MLA](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_formatting_quotations.html) |
| **Chicago 18** | the prose quotation is **five or more lines, or more than 100 words** | [Purdue OWL CMOS 18](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/general_format.html) |

Two MLA details:
- Because MLA counts lines, font and size matter. The MLA Style Center points to its recommendation of a readable typeface at a standard size such as 12 points, so line counts are comparable.
- Purdue OWL notes that if you quote **two or more paragraphs**, MLA uses block format even if the passage is under four lines.

## Formatting

**All three styles:** no quotation marks around the block, which starts on a new line, and the whole block is indented from the left margin.

**APA 7 specifics:**
- Indent the whole block 0.5 in.
- Double-space it, with no extra space before or after.
- For additional paragraphs within the quote, indent each paragraph's first line another 0.5 in.

**MLA 9 specifics:**
- Indent the block half an inch from the left margin.
- Indent every block quotation the same amount. MLA's answer on [indenting block quotations](https://style.mla.org/how-to-indent-block-quotations/) says to do this even after a short introductory line.

**Chicago 18 specifics:**
- Indent with your word processor's indentation tool.
- Match the surrounding text. Purdue OWL notes that a different or smaller font is also an acceptable way to set it off.
- Block quotations, notes, and bibliography entries are exceptions to double-spacing.

## Where the citation goes

**APA:** after the quotation's **final punctuation**, with **no period after** the closing parenthesis. Either:
- put a full parenthetical at the end: `(Author, Year, p. #)`, or
- put author and year in the lead-in sentence and only `(p. #)` at the end.

**MLA:** the parenthetical also comes **after** the block's final punctuation, as in the MLA Style Center's own example ending "...historical events. . . . (Gikandi 3)". In run-in quotations, the citation comes before the period. The MLA Style Center has a [separate answer](https://style.mla.org/the-rules-for-positioning-a-parenthetical-citation-next-to-a-final-period-seem-different-with-run-in-quotations-and-block-quotations-what-is-the-logic-here/) explaining the logic of that difference.

**Chicago NB:** the note reference number goes at the end of the block, after the final punctuation, and the note itself gives the full or shortened citation.

## Side-by-side example

*(Invented passage and source, 50 words.)*

**APA, narrative lead-in:**

> Okafor (2022) described the pattern across all twelve firms:
>
>> Managers in every firm described the first week as a checklist of accounts, trainings, and introductions. Few described it as the start of a working relationship. Where new hires reported feeling integrated, a manager had assigned a real task with a real deadline before the end of the first week. (p. 15)

**MLA:**

> Okafor describes the pattern across all twelve firms:
>
>> Managers in every firm described the first week as a checklist of accounts, trainings, and introductions. Few described it as the start of a working relationship. Where new hires reported feeling integrated, a manager had assigned a real task with a real deadline before the end of the first week. (15)

**Chicago NB:**

> Okafor describes the pattern across all twelve firms:
>
>> Managers in every firm described the first week as a checklist of accounts, trainings, and introductions. Few described it as the start of a working relationship. Where new hires reported feeling integrated, a manager had assigned a real task with a real deadline before the end of the first week.³

At 50 words, this passage is a block in APA (40+ words). In Chicago, it falls under the 100-word threshold, but it may still need a block if it runs to five or more lines on your page. In MLA, it depends on how many lines it fills in your paper. At 12-point type it probably exceeds four lines.

## Judgment calls

- **Should you use a block quote at all?** Block quotes are a strong signal that the exact wording matters. If you can't say why it does, paraphrase. APA in particular prefers paraphrase.
- **Introduce and follow up.** A block quote with no lead-in and no analysis afterward reads as padding. Tell the reader what to notice, then interpret it.
- **Don't trim to dodge the threshold** in a way that changes meaning. Use ellipses honestly, as covered in [changing quotations: ellipses, brackets, and errors](/guides/changing-quotations-ellipses-brackets).
- **Translations:** MLA's answer on [quotations and translations](https://style.mla.org/quotations-and-translations/) (written for the eighth edition) says that if the original is four lines or fewer, run both the original and translation into the text, even if together they exceed four lines.

## Checklist

- [ ] The threshold is measured the way your style measures it (words for APA, lines for MLA, either for Chicago).
- [ ] There are no quotation marks around the block.
- [ ] The whole block is indented consistently.
- [ ] The citation comes after the final punctuation, with no extra period after the parenthetical (APA, MLA).
- [ ] The quoted text matches the source exactly, including original spelling and punctuation.
- [ ] A sentence before the block introduces it, and analysis after it explains it.
