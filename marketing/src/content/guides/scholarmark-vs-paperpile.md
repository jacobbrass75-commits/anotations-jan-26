**Disclosure:** ScholarMark publishes this guide. Paperpile details come from [paperpile.com](https://paperpile.com/) and are limited to what its own pages state. Check its site for current plans and platform support.

Paperpile and ScholarMark both live in the browser and both handle citations, so they show up in the same searches. They're built around different units of work. Paperpile is organized around **papers and references**. ScholarMark is organized around **passages and the arguments they support**.

## What Paperpile says it offers

From Paperpile's site:

- A **browser extension** to "save papers and PDFs from anywhere in one click."
- **Organization** for a large library, plus reading and **annotation** ("highlight what matters").
- A **Google Docs plugin** and a **Word plugin** for "correctly formatted citations and bibliographies as you write."
- **BibTeX and LaTeX** support.
- **Mobile apps** and **shared group libraries** for collaboration.
- Sign-in with a Google account. At the time of writing, new accounts can only be created on Chrome, and Safari and Firefox support is in beta.

If you co-write in Google Docs, or you write in LaTeX, those integrations are the main reason to choose Paperpile.

## What ScholarMark offers

- **Projects** with a stated thesis and scope, folders, and a personal source library you can attach to several projects.
- **Sources:** PDF, TXT, images (including HEIC), and OCR for scans.
- **Highlights filed by purpose:** Key Quote, Evidence, Argument, Methodology, or Your Note, with your note on each.
- **Search** across all sources and annotations in a project.
- **Citations** in Chicago notes-bibliography, MLA, or APA, generated from metadata you save or extract.
- **Drafting** in a writing workspace where quotes that match saved highlights link back to the source passage. Drafts export to DOCX.
- **Verify Paper:** an AI review of the compiled draft that flags quote mismatches, unsupported claims, citations without a matching source, and formatting inconsistencies.
- **A Chrome extension** for clipping selected web text along with the page's URL, title, and available author and date metadata.
- **A connector** so Claude or ChatGPT can save sources and exact-quote highlights into your projects.

"Not stated on Paperpile's pages" means exactly that. Check Paperpile's help center if a feature matters to you.

## Decision criteria

**Pick Paperpile if:**
- You co-write in Google Docs with a lab or advisor.
- You write in LaTeX.
- You manage a large, growing reference library and want it organized by paper.

**Pick ScholarMark if:**
- You work alone or hand off a finished DOCX.
- Your paper leans on direct quotation, as most humanities and many social-science theses do.
- Your sources include scans or photographed archive pages.
- You want the draft and the evidence in the same workspace, with a review pass before you submit.

**Use both if:** you want Paperpile's Google Docs citing for the final document and ScholarMark for quote work and drafting. Keep Paperpile as the source of truth for bibliographic metadata so the two don't drift apart.

## Worked example: a co-authored seminar paper

Two students co-write a 30-page sociology seminar paper in Google Docs, in APA.

**What usually goes wrong:** both of them quote the same interview study, one from the preprint and one from the published version. The wording differs slightly, and the page numbers don't match.

**A Paperpile-centered fix:** keep one shared folder with one record per work, and both cite from the published version through the Docs plugin. This solves the duplicate-record problem directly.

**Where ScholarMark would add something:** if one co-author is drafting the findings section alone, they can highlight the exact passages from the published PDF in ScholarMark, draft the section there, and run Verify Paper on it before pasting into the shared Doc. Then they insert the citations with Paperpile.

For two co-authors working in one Doc, Paperpile is the better center of gravity. ScholarMark is optional, for the person doing quote-heavy drafting.

## Worked example: a solo history thesis

One student, 55 sources (archive scans, monographs, a few articles), Chicago footnotes, and a DOCX submitted to the department.

Here, ScholarMark covers the whole chain: OCR on archive photos, a quote bank with page notes, Chicago full and shortened notes, drafting, review, and DOCX export. Paperpile is optional. You'd add it only if you want plugin-managed citations in Word.
