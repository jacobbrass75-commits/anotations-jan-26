A direct quotation promises the reader that these are the author's exact words. Every style allows small adjustments to fit a quote into your sentence, but the adjustments must be either trivial or visible. This guide covers which is which, and how to avoid most adjustments in the first place.

Sources: APA Style's [changes to quotations](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/quotations/changes) and [quotations](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/quotations) pages, and the MLA Style Center's [How to Avoid Bracketed Changes in Quotations](https://style.mla.org/avoid-bracketed-changes/).

## Changes you can make without marking them (APA)

APA lists four:

1. **Capitalization of the first letter** of the quotation, changed to fit your sentence.
2. **Some end punctuation**, changed to fit your syntax, as long as meaning doesn't change. APA warns that turning a period into a question mark might change the meaning.
3. **Single quotation marks to double**, and vice versa.
4. **Footnote or endnote callouts** in the original, which can be omitted.

Anything else must be marked.

MLA handles capitalization differently in practice. Its own example marks a lowered first letter with brackets: "[l]iterature does not simply...," from its [quotation-within-a-quotation answer](https://style.mla.org/quotation-contained-in-a-quotation/).

## Changes you must mark

**Omitted words: use an ellipsis.**
- APA: three spaced periods (`. . .`) or the ellipsis character (`…`), with a space before and after.
- Use four dots (a period plus an ellipsis) when the omission crosses a sentence boundary.
- Don't put an ellipsis at the start or end of a quotation unless the original has one. APA's quotations page says this explicitly.

**Inserted words: use square brackets**, not parentheses. Use them for a clarifying word, a name in place of a pronoun, or a grammatical adjustment.

**Added emphasis: use italics plus "[emphasis added]"**, placed right after the italicized words (APA's format).

**Errors in the original:** APA says it's "necessary to indicate the error." The conventional marker is "[sic]" right after the error. Don't silently correct it.

## The better move: restructure instead of bracketing

The MLA Style Center argues that bracketed changes are "often distracting and unnecessary," and that you can usually avoid them by changing where the quotation starts or how your sentence is built. Its example uses the first line of *David Copperfield*.

**Bracketed version (unnecessary):**

> At the beginning of the novel, David Copperfield poses a question: "Whether [he] shall turn out to be the hero of [his] own life" (Dickens).

**Without brackets:** quote the original, "Whether I shall turn out to be the hero of my own life." It's already clear who is speaking.

**Bracketed syntax change:**

> For Copperfield, "turn[ing] out to be the hero of my own life" becomes more difficult than might be imagined (Dickens).

**Revised:**

> For Copperfield, becoming "the hero of my own life" is ultimately more difficult than might be imagined (Dickens).

The revision starts the quotation later and lets your sentence do the grammatical work.

## Worked example: three versions of one quotation

*(Invented original, from a 2022 report, p. 15.)*

> **Original:** "In every firm we studied, managers described the first week as a checklist of accounts, trainings, and introductions; few described it as the start of a working relationship."

**Version A: ellipsis, honest.**

> Okafor (2022) found that managers "described the first week as a checklist . . . ; few described it as the start of a working relationship" (p. 15).

The omitted words ("of accounts, trainings, and introductions") are illustrative detail. Cutting them doesn't change the claim.

**Version B: ellipsis, dishonest.**

> Okafor (2022) found that managers "described the first week as . . . the start of a working relationship" (p. 15).

This reverses the meaning. It's technically marked and still a misquotation. **An ellipsis must never change what the author said.**

**Version C: restructured, best.**

> Okafor (2022) found that few managers saw the first week "as the start of a working relationship"; most treated it as "a checklist of accounts, trainings, and introductions" (p. 15).

No brackets and no ellipsis. Two short exact quotations, framed accurately.

## Checklist for every altered quotation

- [ ] Any change beyond APA's four silent changes is marked.
- [ ] No ellipsis at the start or end, unless it's in the original.
- [ ] Every ellipsis can be removed, with the missing words restored, without changing the meaning.
- [ ] Insertions are in square brackets, not parentheses.
- [ ] Added emphasis is labeled.
- [ ] Errors in the original are kept and marked, not fixed.
- [ ] You tried restructuring first.
