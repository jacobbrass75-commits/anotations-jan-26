**Disclosure:** ScholarMark publishes this guide and is one of the options below. Everything said about the other tools comes from their own websites, linked inline.

Before switching away from Zotero, name the gap. Zotero is free, open source, and covers the core reference-manager job well: capture from the browser, organize, annotate PDFs, and cite live in Word, LibreOffice, and Google Docs ([Zotero docs](https://www.zotero.org/support/word_processor_integration)). Most people who search for "Zotero alternatives" don't need a different reference manager. They need a tool for a job Zotero wasn't designed for.

Find your gap below.

## Gap 1: "My institution pays for something else" or "my lab standardizes on it"

**EndNote** (Clarivate). Its [site](https://endnote.com/) lists over 7,000 citation styles, cloud sync, team libraries, and inserting citations and bibliographies as you write. Universities often license it, so check your library's software page first.

**Mendeley** (Elsevier). Described as a [free reference manager](https://www.elsevier.com/products/mendeley) that stores, organizes, notes, shares, and cites references, with bibliography generation and import from other research software.

*Switch if:* your advisor, lab, or co-authors already work in one of these. A shared tool is worth more than a marginally better one.

*Keep in mind:* moving libraries between reference managers is usually possible but rarely perfectly clean. Spot-check a sample of migrated records for authors, dates, and attachments.

## Gap 2: "I write everything in Google Docs and want it to feel native"

**Paperpile.** [Paperpile](https://paperpile.com/) has a Google Docs plugin, a Word plugin, a browser extension, mobile apps, and BibTeX/LaTeX support. At the time of writing, new sign-ups are Chrome-only, with Safari and Firefox in beta.

*Switch if:* Google Docs collaboration is the center of your workflow. Note that Zotero also has a [Google Docs integration](https://www.zotero.org/support/google_docs), so compare both on your own document before moving.

## Gap 3: "I can format citations fine. I lose track of the quotes."

This is the gap ScholarMark targets. Reference managers are organized around bibliographic records. A long paper, though, tends to fail at the passage level: the quote you remembered slightly wrong, the page you can't find again, the paraphrase that drifted from what the author actually wrote.

**ScholarMark** organizes work by project. You upload PDFs, text, or image scans. You highlight passages and file each one as Key Quote, Evidence, Argument, Methodology, or Your Note, with a note about how you'll use it. You search across the project, draft from that evidence, and run an AI "Verify Paper" review that flags quotes and citations that don't match your sources. Citation output covers Chicago notes-bibliography, MLA, and APA.

*Switch if:* you quote heavily, especially from archives, scans, or long books, and you want drafting and evidence in one place.

*Keep in mind:* ScholarMark has no Word or Google Docs plugin, no BibTeX/RIS import, and only three styles. Many people keep Zotero for final citations and use ScholarMark for the quote work. That split is covered in [ScholarMark vs Zotero](/guides/scholarmark-vs-zotero).

## Gap 4: "I want to ask questions about my PDFs"

**NotebookLM (Gemini Notebook).** Google's help center now calls NotebookLM "Gemini Notebook." It [accepts](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16215270?hl=en) PDFs, Word files, URLs, YouTube links, audio, and more, with up to 50 sources per notebook for free users. Answers carry in-line citations to your sources.

*Add it if:* you want to understand a reading set quickly. It complements a reference manager rather than replacing one.

*Keep in mind:* in-line references to a source passage aren't the same as formatted Chicago or APA citations. See [ScholarMark vs NotebookLM](/guides/scholarmark-vs-notebooklm) for where each fits.

## Gap 5: "I need to find papers, not store them"

**Elicit** [searches](https://elicit.com/) over 138 million papers and 545,000 clinical trials and produces structured research reports. **scite** [shows](https://scite.ai/) how later papers have supported, challenged, or discussed a finding, and it even offers a Zotero plugin.

*Add these if:* discovery is your bottleneck. They feed a library; they don't replace one. More in [Elicit, scite, and ScholarMark](/guides/scholarmark-vs-elicit-and-scite).

## Quick decision table

| Your gap | Try | Replace Zotero? |
|---|---|---|
| Institution or lab standard | EndNote, Mendeley | Possibly |
| Google Docs-first writing | Paperpile (or Zotero's Docs plugin) | Possibly |
| Losing track of exact quotes | ScholarMark | Usually alongside Zotero |
| Questions about your reading | NotebookLM / Gemini Notebook | No, add it |
| Finding new literature | Elicit, scite, Google Scholar | No, add it |

## Worked example: diagnosing your own gap

Here's a test you can run in 15 minutes. Open your current draft and pick five direct quotations at random. For each, time how long it takes to:

1. Find the exact passage in the source.
2. Confirm the wording matches character for character.
3. Confirm the page number in your citation matches the printed page.

**If each takes under a minute** and everything matches, your system works. You may only need a better citation workflow, if anything.

**If you can't find two or more passages quickly,** or the wording has drifted, your gap is quote tracking (Gap 3), not reference management. Switching reference managers won't fix it.

**If finding was easy but the citations were formatted inconsistently,** stay with a reference manager and fix your style settings.

## What not to do

- **Don't migrate mid-thesis without a reason.** Migration costs a weekend and can introduce metadata errors right before a deadline.
- **Don't run two reference managers in parallel.** If you add a second tool, give it a different job (quotes, discovery, Q&A), not the same one.
- **Don't drop Zotero just to try AI.** Most AI tools add to a reference manager rather than replace it.
