In a Chicago notes-bibliography paper, most of your notes are repeat citations: the same book or article, cited again on a different page. Getting those right is mostly a matter of knowing three forms and when each applies.

Sources: Purdue OWL's [CMOS 18th edition overview](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/chicago_manual_of_style_18th_edition.html) and the University of Chicago Press [citation quick guide](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide.html). If your department follows the 17th edition, ask whether it has updated.

## The three forms

**1. The full note.** Used the first time you cite a source, unless your paper has a bibliography and you choose the short form from the start (see below).

> 1. Maya Ortiz, *Harbor Cities: Trade and Labor on the Gulf Coast, 1880–1920* (Riverbend University Press, 2019), 142.

**2. The shortened note.** Author's surname, a shortened title if the title is more than four words, and page number.

> 4. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 157.

**3. Ibid.** Means "in the same place." Purdue's summary of CMOS 18: although ibid. is "still discouraged," it's acceptable when you cite the same source in consecutive notes. Use "Ibid." alone for the same page, or "Ibid., 160." for a different page.

(The Ortiz book is invented for illustration.)

## What CMOS 18 recommends

From Purdue OWL's summary of the 18th edition:

- **Shortened notes are the default for repeats**, "including when cited two or more times consecutively." So the preferred way to cite Ortiz twice in a row is two shortened notes, not "Ibid."
- **In a paper with a bibliography, the first citation can already be short:** author, title, page. Readers find the full details in the bibliography.
- **After that, later notes may drop the title** and give only author and page, for example "Ortiz, 157."
- **Without a bibliography,** repeat the full note the first time a source appears in each new chapter.

That third point is the easiest to misapply. Author-only short notes only work when no two cited works share an author surname. If you cite two books by Ortiz, or works by two different Ortizes, keep the titles.

## Worked example: one paragraph, four notes

Your paragraph cites Ortiz three times and a journal article once. The paper has a bibliography.

**Conservative approach (full first note, shortened repeats):**

> 1. Maya Ortiz, *Harbor Cities: Trade and Labor on the Gulf Coast, 1880–1920* (Riverbend University Press, 2019), 142.
> 2. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 145.
> 3. Daniel Price, "Wage Floors on the Waterfront," *Journal of Gulf Labor History* 12, no. 2 (2021): 77.
> 4. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 151.

**With "Ibid." (acceptable, discouraged):**

> 2. Ibid., 145.

Note 4 can't be "Ibid." because note 3 cites a different source in between.

**CMOS 18 short-first approach (only because there's a bibliography):**

> 1. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 142.
> 2. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 145.
> 3. Price, "Wage Floors," 77.
> 4. Ortiz, *Harbor Cities*, 151.

Article titles stay in quotation marks in short notes, and book titles stay italicized.

## Choosing a short title

The rule is "a shortened form of the title (if more than four words)." In practice:

- Keep the words a reader would recognize. *Harbor Cities* is better than *Harbor Cities: Trade and*.
- Drop subtitles.
- Keep the title's formatting: italics for books, quotation marks for articles and chapters.
- Be consistent. Use the same short title every time you cite that work.

## Why ibid. causes trouble in long drafts

Ibid. depends on note order. Move a paragraph, add a note between two others, or cut a citation, and an "Ibid." can silently start pointing to the wrong source. Shortened notes describe the source itself, so they survive revision. That's a practical reason, beyond style preference, to avoid ibid. until your final draft, if you use it at all.

## Quick checklist for repeat notes

- [ ] Every source's first appearance is either a full note or (with a bibliography) a short note that matches a bibliography entry.
- [ ] Each short title is used consistently.
- [ ] No "Ibid." follows a note citing a different source.
- [ ] Author-only short notes appear only where no other cited author shares the surname.
- [ ] Page numbers are the printed pages of the edition in your bibliography (see [page numbers for PDFs, scans, and e-books](/guides/citing-page-numbers-pdfs-scans-ebooks)).
