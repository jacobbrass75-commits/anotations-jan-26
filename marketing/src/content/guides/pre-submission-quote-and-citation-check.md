Proofreading catches typos. It doesn't catch a misquoted sentence, a citation to the wrong page, or a bibliography entry for a source you cut three drafts ago. Those need a separate check that treats quotations and citations as data to verify, not prose to polish.

Here are seven passes. Run them in order, since each one assumes the previous one is done, and run them on a **frozen** draft. If you're still revising, you'll be checking twice.

## Pass 1: Inventory

List every:
- direct quotation (search for `"`, `“`, and block-indented passages),
- citation (in-text parentheticals or footnote numbers),
- bibliography or reference entry.

Write the counts down. They're your progress bar, and the starting point for Pass 6.

## Pass 2: Quote wording

For **every** direct quotation, compare your text with the source text word for word. Check for:

- swapped, dropped, or added words ("not" is the dangerous one),
- spelling and punctuation that should be preserved from the original,
- ellipses that change meaning, and brackets used properly (see [changing quotations honestly](/guides/changing-quotations-ellipses-brackets)),
- quotation marks that open and close in the right places.

**Tip:** search the source for a distinctive three- or four-word fragment, not the whole quote. Small differences defeat a full-sentence search, and those differences are exactly what you're looking for.

## Pass 3: Locators

For every page-cited quotation or paraphrase, confirm the number is the **printed page** of the edition in your bibliography. The usual failures are PDF viewer pages, preprint pagination, and quotes that run across two pages. See [page numbers for PDFs, scans, and e-books](/guides/citing-page-numbers-pdfs-scans-ebooks).

## Pass 4: Claim support

You probably can't re-read every source in full. Prioritize instead:

- **Every claim in your thesis statement and topic sentences** that rests on a citation.
- **Every number or statistic.**
- **Every citation an AI tool suggested**, even ones you checked earlier. Re-check them against [the five-level procedure](/guides/how-to-verify-ai-generated-citations).
- **A random sample** of the rest, perhaps one in five. If the sample turns up problems, widen it.

For each, open the cited passage and confirm that it says what your sentence says, at the same strength and scope. If you haven't mapped which claims rest on which evidence, a [reverse outline evidence audit](/guides/reverse-outline-evidence-audit) of each section is the fastest way to find the claims to prioritize.

## Pass 5: Style format

Now check formatting against your style manual:

- in-text or note format (author-date, MLA parenthetical, or Chicago note),
- first vs shortened notes in Chicago, and any stray "Ibid." after a cut or reordered note (see [Chicago shortened notes and ibid.](/guides/chicago-shortened-notes-ibid-cmos-18)),
- block quotation thresholds and formatting (see [block quote rules](/guides/block-quote-rules-apa-mla-chicago)),
- consistency: the same work cited the same way every time.

## Pass 6: Bibliography reconciliation

Two checks, in both directions:

1. **Every citation has an entry.** Go through citations in order and tick off the matching entry.
2. **Every entry has a citation.** Any entry left unticked is a source you no longer cite. Remove it, unless your style or instructor allows a broader "works consulted" list.

Also check that the edition, year, and pages in each entry match what you actually used in Pass 3.

## Pass 7: Policy and disclosure

- Re-read the assignment's rules on sources and AI use.
- If disclosure of AI assistance is required, write it accurately: which tool, for what, and which parts of the work it touched.
- Keep your notes and drafts. If you're ever asked how you used sources, they're your record.

## Worked example: a 25-page seminar paper

**Inventory:** 31 direct quotations, 54 citations, 29 bibliography entries.

**Pass 2:** 3 quotations had wording drift: one dropped "often," one changed "may" to "will," and one had a typo introduced while pasting. All three were fixed.

**Pass 3:** 2 citations used PDF viewer pages. Corrected to the printed pages.

**Pass 4:** 14 claim-support checks (topic sentences, all statistics, 3 AI-suggested citations, and a random 5). One AI-suggested citation supported a related claim, not the one in the paper. It was replaced with a better source, and the sentence was narrowed.

**Pass 5:** one "Ibid." pointed to the wrong source after a paragraph was moved. It was converted to a shortened note.

**Pass 6:** 2 bibliography entries were no longer cited. Both were removed. 1 citation had no entry, a source added late. An entry was added.

**Result:** 9 fixes, none of which a spell-checker would have found.

## When something fails and you can't fix it

- **Quote can't be found in the source:** don't use it as a quotation. Find the real wording, paraphrase from what you can verify, or cut the claim.
- **Source can't be accessed to re-check:** if the claim is minor, consider cutting it. If it's central, ask a librarian today.
- **Out of time:** prioritize Passes 2 and 4 for your central claims. A misquoted thesis-supporting sentence is worse than a misplaced comma in a bibliography entry.

## Using ScholarMark for the check

If you drafted in ScholarMark, quotations that match saved highlights link back to their source passages, which speeds up Pass 2. **Verify Paper** runs an AI review of a compiled draft against your project sources. It's designed to cross-check direct quotes, check paraphrase accuracy, check page references where provided, flag citations with no matching source, check footnote numbering and style consistency, and flag over-claimed assertions. The report lists findings by severity, each with a location, the issue, and a suggested fix.

Two honest caveats:
- **Very long papers** have to be verified **section by section**. ScholarMark refuses to verify a paper too large for one pass, so that no source check gets skipped.
- **It's a second reader, not a substitute** for Passes 2–4. Check each finding against the source yourself, and don't assume an unflagged sentence is correct.
